# Product development phases

The PM confirms the active phase based on the next product outcome to prove, not only on repository maturity.

## Phase 0 — Research & Business Planning

Purpose: decide whether there is a sufficiently credible problem/opportunity and product/business thesis to justify a Demo.

Typical exit evidence: customer/problem evidence, alternatives/market understanding, target segment, business-model hypothesis, technical-feasibility risks, proposed Demo thesis, and the complete bilingual English/Farsi proposal.

## Phase 1 — Demo

Purpose: demonstrate the core value proposition convincingly enough to justify building a real MVP.

Typical exit evidence: the riskiest product/technical assumptions have been demonstrated with a deliberately limited solution and relevant users/stakeholders can understand the value.

## Phase 2 — MVP

Purpose: prove that real target users can successfully use a minimally complete product in realistic conditions.

Typical exit evidence: end-to-end user success, real data/operations as required, initial reliability/support expectations, and evidence about retention/value rather than only a technical deployment.

## Phase 3 — V1

Purpose: make the product reliable and complete enough for repeatable commercial or organizational use beyond a narrow pilot.

Typical exit evidence: hardened core workflows, onboarding, analytics/operations, security/reliability appropriate to the product, and evidence that the product can be repeatedly sold/deployed/supported.

## Phase 4 — Full Product

Purpose: operate and evolve the mature product sustainably at its intended scale.

Typical exit evidence is product-specific and may involve scalability, portfolio/segment expansion, operational maturity, platform capabilities, enterprise requirements, or other long-term goals. Phase 4 is not "done forever"; it is the ongoing mature-product phase.

## Phase gate rule

A phase completes when its declared exit criteria have evidence and the PM explicitly chooses a transition. An empty backlog is not an exit criterion unless the PM deliberately made it one.
